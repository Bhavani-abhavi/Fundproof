## 1. Purpose and intended use

- Estimates, for one motor third-party liability policy, the expected number of claims per policy-year (frequency), the expected average claim amount on a capped basis (severity) and their product, the expected loss per policy-year.
- Intended use: decision support and stress testing in the Underwriting Copilot (`/score` with scenario overrides, risk bands and reason codes).
- Not for: rate filings, pricing a real book, or any portfolio other than the public freMTPL2 data.

## 2. Materiality

- Portfolio exposure: not measured (no real portfolio; demonstration on public data).
- Consumer impact if used for pricing or underwriting: high (personal lines), which the NAIC bulletin asks insurers to weigh when scaling controls.
- Complexity: GLMs are standard actuarial practice; LightGBM adds flexibility and needs SHAP for explanations.

## 3. Data

- Source: freMTPL2freq (OpenML 41214), public French motor third-party liability data; severity: freMTPL2sev (OpenML 41215) joined to freMTPL2freq on IDpol.
- After cleaning: 678,013 policies, 358,360 policy-years, 36,056 claims after the claim-count cap of 4.
- Claims without a recorded amount: 9,116 policies carry 9,650 claims (26.8% of claims) with no row in the severity file; 9,094 of them sit in a block of IDpol below 24,952 where every policy has a claim. Those claims are not counted for modelling (see docs/insurance_data_notes.md and the validation report).
- Claim amounts vs the capped claim count: 9 policies have a number of claim amounts different from the modelling ClaimNb (39 amount rows above it, 1 modelled claim(s) without an amount row; listed in docs/insurance_data_notes.md), so on those policies the observed loss and the frequency target do not count exactly the same claims.
- Target: ClaimNb per policy (capped at 4; claims without a recorded amount not counted), with log(Exposure) offset; rates are claims per policy-year.
- Split: random, stratified on ClaimNb > 0 (sklearn train_test_split, twice), seed 42. Out-of-time test: not measured (freMTPL2 has no date or policy-year field, so an out-of-time test is impossible. Train, validation and test are random draws from the same period; the test metrics do not show how the models would hold up on a later year.).

## 4. Methodology

- Frequency GLM: Poisson GLM, log link, offset log(Exposure) (statsmodels IRLS); 56 parameters; banded ages and power so each coefficient reads as a relativity.
- Frequency LightGBM: LightGBM objective=poisson, init_score = log(Exposure) + log(train claim frequency); 325 trees chosen by early stopping on the validation split; other settings fixed in advance (no tuning).
- Severity GLM: Gamma GLM, log link, var_weights = number of claims (statsmodels IRLS).
- Severity LightGBM: LightGBM objective=gamma, weight = number of claims, init_score = log(train mean severity); 37 trees.
- Expected loss: predicted loss per policy = predicted frequency (claims/year) x exposure (years) x predicted severity (capped amount per claim); totals over all test policies.

## 8. Fairness testing

freMTPL2 has no protected attributes, so an adverse impact ratio cannot be computed for these models: not measured. Region, Area and Density are geographic rating features that can act as proxies for protected characteristics. If a model like this were an AI system used for New York underwriting or pricing, NY DFS Circular Letter No. 7 would expect a comprehensive, quantitative unfair-discrimination assessment (for example an adverse impact ratio); its proxy-assessment provision is written for external consumer data and information sources, whereas Area, Region and Density are traditional rating variables (and freMTPL2 is French data). | Population stability of the predicted frequency | PSI against the training population, per period once dated data exists | PSI <= 0.10 stable; 0.10 to 0.25 moderate shift (investigate); > 0.25 significant shift (flag) | Flag goes to the model owner; a flag on the score triggers a review of calibration and of the features that moved |

## 11. Limitations

- freMTPL2 has no date or policy-year field, so an out-of-time test is impossible. Train, validation and test are random draws from the same period; the test metrics do not show how the models would hold up on a later year.
- Population stability (PSI) across time is not measured, for the same reason.
- The data has no customer or vehicle identifier beyond IDpol, so the same insured risk can sit in train and test: 26.5% of test policies share every rating feature with a training policy. grouped_split_sensitivity refits both frequency models on a split grouped on the nine rating features (0.0% shared): LightGBM minus GLM mean Poisson deviance is -0.0108 [-0.0123, -0.0093] there against -0.0091 [-0.0107, -0.0075] on the random split, so the conclusion of the model comparison does not depend on identical risks sitting in both train and test.
- The frequency target counts only claims with a recorded amount (see docs/insurance_data_notes.md), so frequency numbers are not directly comparable with published freMTPL2 results that use the raw ClaimNb.
- LightGBM hyperparameters were fixed in advance and not tuned; only the number of trees is chosen, by early stopping on the validation split.
- The validation split that early-stops LightGBM is also the one that chooses between GLM and LightGBM, which slightly favours LightGBM in that choice. The paired comparison on the untouched test split is the unbiased check of the choice; a cleaner design would early-stop on a separate fold of the training data.
- Claim amounts are capped at the 99.5th percentile of training claims, so expected loss is on a capped basis with no large-loss loading.
- Results are measured on public historical data from one French motor portfolio. They say nothing about performance on any other portfolio or in production.
