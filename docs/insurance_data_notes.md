## Policies with claims but no claim amounts

9,116 policies with `ClaimNb > 0` have no row in the severity
file. They carry 9,650 claims (after the cap), which is
26.76% of all claims and
26.76% of policies with claims.

Where they sit: every policy with IDpol below 24,952 has at least one claim (9,387 policies, against 3.69% of policies with a claim from that IDpol on), and 9,094 of them have no claim amounts. This claimant-only block holds 9,935 claims, 27.55% of all claims. A random sample of policies in which about 3.69% have a claim would essentially never produce such a run, so our reading is that the block is an artifact of how the public files were assembled, not a set of claims that cost nothing. The table shows the same pattern by IDpol band.

| IDpol band | Policies | Policies with claims | ... with amounts | ... without amounts |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1-10,000 | 4,548 | 4,548 | 94 | 4,454 |
| 10,001-30,000 | 7,645 | 4,966 | 325 | 4,641 |
| 30,001-100,000 | 37,139 | 2,152 | 2,151 | 1 |
| 100,001-1,000,000 | 37,698 | 1,822 | 1,819 | 3 |
| above 1,000,000 | 590,983 | 20,572 | 20,555 | 17 |

Treatment (a judgement call, stated so it can be challenged): as in the scikit-learn example "Tweedie regression on insurance claims", a claim with no recorded amount is not counted as a claim for modelling. `ClaimNb` is set to 0 on 9,116 policies (9,650 claims); the capped count stays in `ClaimNbCapped` and the file value in `ClaimNbRecorded`. The modelling target then has 26,406 claims, 0.0737 claims per policy-year (against 0.1006 as recorded). Frequency and severity now describe the same claims, except on 9 policies whose number of claim amounts differs from the capped claim count (the cleaning table lists them), so expected loss (frequency x severity) can be compared directly with observed losses. After this step, 3.12% of the policies in the claimant-only block have a claim, close to the 3.69% of policies with a recorded claim in the rest of the file, which supports the reading that the claims without amounts are an artifact.

Why not keep them as claims? `recorded_claim_count_sensitivity` in `results/risk_metrics.json` refits both frequency models on the recorded counts. On the 133,688 test policies outside the claimant-only block, observed / predicted claims is then 0.743 (GLM) and 0.756 (LightGBM): those models predict too many claims for ordinary policies, because they learn from the block. With the chosen treatment the same ratio is 1.007 (GLM) and 1.005 (LightGBM). These are point estimates from a sensitivity check, not headline metrics.

The headline frequency metrics are also recomputed without the claimant-only block (`claimant_only_block_sensitivity`): the LightGBM minus GLM difference in mean Poisson deviance is -0.0091 (95% CI -0.0107 to -0.0075).

## Split

Seeded (42) random split, stratified on `ClaimNb > 0`, 60% / 20% / 20%.
freMTPL2 has no date or policy-year field, so an out-of-time test is impossible. Train, validation and test are random draws from the same period; the test metrics do not show how the models would hold up on a later year.

| test | 135,603 | 71,461 | 5,295 | 3.68% | 4,989 |

## Large-loss cap (severity)

Each claim amount is capped at the 99.5th percentile of the training split's claim
amounts: 34,328.39. In the training split this caps 80 of
15,833 claims (0.51%) and removes
29.33% of the total claim amount. In the test split it caps
26 of 5,298 claims
(0.49%), removing 12.13% of the
amount. Expected loss is therefore on a capped basis; pricing the removed large losses would need a separate
loading, which is not built here.
